# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The identity is now its own stage (#45). The dealt screens are look probes: never polished. After the pick, `design` decides the identity in small rendered steps: faces, three colours only (ground, text colour with the dealt ink first, one accent; every other role derived by `colours.mjs --roles` and checked), a wordmark round, and three or four hero concepts, each one idea with exactly one hero (`grid.html?concept=`). The bento board, built as a web page from those picks by the board rules `design` already had, is the "this is us" checkpoint; a note reopens only that round. Only then is the home screen finished, the other screens prototyped, and the result set beside a plain agent's screen in the same identity, which it must clearly beat. The Figma board rebuilds the approved one. A pool under about 100 keeps takes a light funnel: no tagging or families script, grouped by eye.
- From a full run on a 1,300-card Figma board (#45): the first screen now starts from what it must carry. Before the deal, `design` lists what the brief needs on it (status, the main content, the primary and secondary actions, where to find it), offers three or four headlines that say the takeaway outright, and proposes the ownable; all three are the same content on every dealt screen, and the grid page flags any must-carry item an arrangement leaves out. The library gains `controls` (buttons, a status chip, a list row), so actions have a hierarchy. Arrangements can be made of zones, and `deal.mjs --mix 7,zone.side=4` takes one zone from another screen as a dealt move. A grid screen is a sketch: comparisons wait until it is finished.
- Big Figma boards: the agent asks whether to read the whole file, reads your captions, tags and stars from the board, and downloads each shared image sheet once and crops the cards locally (`crops.mjs`). Non-screens on a moodboard are marked and never lead quietly. `measure.mjs` reads a screen shown on a grey or black presentation frame inside the frame. `families.mjs` splits a family far bigger than the rest on its own and says so, shows the largest family next to the nearby spreads, and takes `--only families/fN.tsv` to sort one family again.
- On the rendered screen: `screen-contrast.mjs` samples under every text box, the fault pass also catches repeated copy and lone last words, every offset goes through `--space` so the spacing row really changes the screen, stand-in pictures show the product's own subject, and the finished screen keeps the grid template and its overrides.
- Fixes: `capture.mjs` fails on a page it cannot reach instead of saving Chrome's error page, dismisses more consent banners, and keeps a long local page in parts; `fonts.mjs --kind` overrides Google's category; `colours.mjs` keeps the dealt ink wherever it reads.
- The agent no longer chooses the look, and no longer asks you to describe one. `design-direction` sorts the references (your moodboard, or what you liked in the picker) into families that look alike, as many as the pool has looks. On one board you mark, in the family you want, one lead (the anchor) and the likes (the donors). The agent reads those into `library.json`: how their screens are arranged, how they do navigation, and what type, grounds and accents they use. This replaces the three proposed directions, each anchored on one reference, and the moodboard grouped by part.
- `design` deals eight first screens from that library and builds each with your real content. A recipe's arrangement, type and ground always come from three different references, so no reference can be copied whole. The anchor's arrangement appears twice. You pick from one numbered grid, see each pick varied one thing at a time (navigation, type, ground, accent), and pick again. Only then is the screen finished and the other key screens built. This replaces the questions on colour and type, and the two or three variants of every key screen.
- The agent says what each screen is made of and lists faults it could not fix. It does not rank the screens or say which it prefers.
- New scripts: `measure.mjs` reads an image's colours or a live page's fonts, sizes and colours, so the library is measured, not judged by eye. `deal.mjs` deals the recipes and the variations and keeps every round. `sheet.mjs` lays the screenshots out as one numbered sheet.
- Families are sorted on numbers first. Every kept reference is measured before grouping (how light, how empty, how loud, and on a live page the type scale, how much is pictures, the margin and the radius), each tile shows its numbers, and each family shows its ranges. A family with a wide range is split or explained. A note "move to <family>" on a tile moves it.
- What a family shares is kept in the library as `family` and applied to every dealt screen, so eight different screens stay one look. Type entries keep their display size and the body face they were paired with.
- Big moodboards are no longer cut to 20–60. Every fit is kept and measured in one browser run (60 images took 1.4 s against 31.5 s with a browser per image). `families.mjs` proposes families, as many as there are looks, and writes the board. The eye checks them from contact sheets, not picture by picture, and full size is kept for the anchor and the donors. Over about 60 keeps, you first take families on an overview board (`moodboard.mjs --overview 6`), then mark the ones you took.
- Before the picker, the agent checks the whole pool on contact sheets and drops only what is not a screen or is broken, never a look. If that leaves the pool thin, it searches again rather than showing less.
- Gradients and patterns are measured and kept, never judged: a family that relies on a gradient keeps it in its library, with the CSS as measured. A dot grid is recorded as a pattern, and a see-through gradient as neither. Search no longer drops a reference for a look the rules call a tell.
- Families are sorted on layout too (how much is picture, how much gradient, whether the headline is centred), and a family the agent can only name by a number is broken up as a bin.
- Every reference gets a profile in one local file, `profiles.json`: its measured traits; its figure (how many colours, how colourful, how grey, soft surfaces against hard edges, its palette, and a 6 by 4 grid of where things sit), which tells a photograph from a 3D render from chrome lettering; what its page is made of (images, video, canvas, SVG), its fonts, largest texts, gradients and patterns; and the agent's tags in a fixed vocabulary (picture, photo, type, weight, layout, colour, surface) with an optional note. `measure.mjs --links --profiles` builds it in one browser run, and `tag.mjs` makes the tagging sheets and checks every word. `families.mjs` sorts on all of it: each kind of trait is a distance from 0 to 1, measured traits hold two thirds of the weight and tags a third, a wide gap on any trait seen first (how empty, how loud, type size, where things sit, what the picture is, the layout) keeps two references apart, and an untagged one is compared on its numbers only and named. It joins the closest first, so outliers stay apart as strays; it names pairs of strays as family seeds and each stray's nearest family, folds two shots of one site into one reference, says what a slightly lower and higher `--spread` would give, and leads each family's line with what its tags agree on.
- Pictures are told apart in more kinds: the tags add icon (small symbols, logos, emoji) and shape (solid flat shapes, colour blocks), and each page counts its icons and how much of it is solid blocks. Light or dark no longer splits a family: the same look on both grounds is one family, and the ground is dealt later.
- More places to steer before design starts: the families are presented with what the agent's eye changed in each, and which ones look like your "never feel" (a hint, never a cut); pairs of strays are their own groups to take as seeds; taking no family leads to a search from your words; likes outside the lead's family are asked about; and the library is shown in a short readout, stand-in fonts and gradients flagged, for you to drop or swap entries before the direction is written.
- After a screen is chosen, you tune its colour and type on it: rows of display faces, body faces, grounds and accents rendered on your screen, from close alternatives, the family's own palettes and anything you name, each contrast-checked before you see it. Pick, keep, or name your own; the agent never picks or ranks.
- The other key screens are prototyped in one go in your chosen look, not chosen one by one: the agent confirms the list, builds them linked to click through from references that show each kind of screen (any family, since the look is fixed), and names every fork. Only at a fork, or where you ask, does `deal.mjs --part` deal two to four layouts in your look and the screens chosen so far; `--choose` records each pick, and a layout's `needs` keeps out one that would not fit what was chosen.
- `design` ships a grid page template (`references/grid-template.html`): it loads the deal, sets the look as variables, takes colour and face overrides and a phone size in its URL, loads each face on its own (or from a stylesheet the library names, for Fontshare faces and variable-font axes), measures type by its text so a row cannot stretch it, keeps one size per line, and signals when it is ready. A run writes only the content, the navigations and the arrangements.
- A `scale` tag (quiet, heading, large, huge) records how big the largest type is, so a moodboard of pictures alone, with no live pages to measure, still sorts on it.
- Fewer places where the agent chooses for you: every like is kept (only broken ones are dropped, into a `Dropped` group on the board you can bring back); the grid comes with a sheet of the references each arrangement was read from; step 5's colour rows come from `colours.mjs` (even OKLCH steps, the family's colours and yours, each with its ink, failing contrast left out); spacing is a row of its own (`&space=` on the grid page).
- The first questions are a studio's brief round, in one message, each with a suggested answer from your brief: what someone should think or do in five seconds, personality sliders (quiet–loud, type-led–picture-led, plain–rich, warm–cool), how it should feel and never feel, who it should stand apart from, what is already fixed (colours, faces, logo, light or dark, your pictures), and your moodboard or where to look. `families.mjs --sliders` places each family on the sliders from what is measured and tagged, and the families board says which sit near what you asked and which are off, as a hint. What is fixed is asked here, not at the start of design, so it shapes the library; design only checks it in one line. The round also asks the one action the first screen leads to, what you tried before and did not like, whether to fit in with the category or stand out, the content's limits (languages, how much, accessibility), and who else has to say yes. Where what you chose sits far from what you said on a slider, the families step asks once which should lead.
- Step 5's face rows come from `fonts.mjs`, not the agent's memory: every face of the same kind in Google Fonts' list (or, for a face from elsewhere, the kind you give and its stylesheet) is measured in one Chrome (width, x-height, darkness, stroke contrast) and listed nearest in shape first, with how widely each is used. At least two of the five shown come from beyond the hundred most used.
- Live pages read better: a headline split into word spans with no box of its own is read whole, animations that end are read at their end, not mid-way, and text hidden by a wrapper is not counted. A page that is not the real site (an error status, a maintenance or bot-check page, an empty page) is flagged; `families.mjs` sorts that reference on its picture alone and names it, for the eye to confirm and drop.
- The library readout and the direction are one message with one question, not two stops. A second look (leads in two families) gets its own folder, `identity-2/`, and design works on the one you named first. The screen you settle on is recorded with `--choose`, and step 5 mixes into it with `--mix chosen,...`.
- The flow picture shows the picker round for a gap search, the read-back after variations and after colour and type, `DESIGN.md` before the Figma board and the board's own yes, and that "go" at any black step lets the agent decide.
- In the grid, a variation can be varied again, a mix (`--mix 7,ground=3`) and more of one thing (`--more 4:type`) are dealt by the script, and the anchor's two screens never sit side by side.
- The brief no longer describes a look. `idea` keeps layout, colour, typeface and named styles out of it.
- A device that came with a look you chose is on purpose: `DESIGN.md` says what it does for the product, and the design rules no longer count it as a tell.
- A `direction.md` from an earlier version has no library beside it. `design` sends it back to `design-direction`, which picks up at the families with the references it already has.

## 0.11.2 — 2026-11-18

- `design-direction` asks whether you already have a moodboard: a Figma link (file, page or section), a folder of images, or links. If you do, it is the base. The agent reads the board and checks whether it is enough: three distinct anchors, and every part of the product covered. If it is, there is no search. If not, it says what the board lacks and offers a search for only that (a pool of 20 to 60, one picker round), or the full search when the board is too thin for even one direction. It opens the shortlisted references at full size (from Figma, the original upload, GIFs included, with motion recorded as frames), and proposes 3 directions as small boards of 6 to 8 references. Each direction is anchored on one reference's move, kept whole, with moves from other references combined around it and never averaged, and never one reference's whole concept. The search, the picker and the board of 100 stay for people without a moodboard.
- `design` keeps the direction's reference shots open while prototyping, for the finish text cannot carry: type character, spacing, texture. They rank below the design rules and are never copied. A reference that moves is replayed before the part it inspires is prototyped: its frames, and its live page recorded again, so the prototype's motion has the same feel.

## 0.11.1 — 2026-11-17

- The brief has a **Position** section: what the product is up against, the one concrete thing it is remembered for, and its personality. `idea` recommends it from what people do today instead, and `design-direction` builds `ownable`, `voice` and `leave` from it, so the look aims at being distinctive rather than guessing.
- `design-direction` keeps about 100 references by default instead of 12, spread across the whole site (home, navigation, index, case study, article, blog, photography, motion) or an app's key screens, and reaches back to older award winners. The pool and the second round grow to match, and a keep is the page that shows its part, not just the home page.
- The moodboard is a picker. Tiles are grouped by part, each with Lead (one per part), Like, No and a note, and a copy-out for the chat. `direction.md` lists its references by part. Recorded motion plays on the tile.
- `design` lays the approved look out as a bento board in Figma before any component: the name in the display face, the type styles, the colour tokens and themes, real pictures from the prototype, a line in the voice, a key screen, the ownable, and a big number if the product has one. Every tile is bound to the tokens, and they say yes to it before the screens are built.
- `capture.mjs` waits out loaders and intros (no loader covering the screen, not one flat colour, the network quiet; up to 15 s on the web, 2 s locally, `--wait` to change), turns down cookie banners without removing the page around them, and keeps the first screen when a canvas page comes back blank at full length. `--frames N` records timed frames of an intro, a scroll (`--scroll`) or a click (`--click`), and `--click` alone captures the screen after a click, such as an open menu. Local pages are left as they are (no consent clicks), and a local file can be captured up to 30000 px tall, so a moodboard of 100 fits. WebGL and intro-heavy sites came back blank before, and transitions could not be judged from stills.

## 0.11.0 — 2026-11-15

- `design-direction` calls itself the inspiration step, and its wording and the README's say so plainly.
- A project that joins with some stages done no longer gets sent back through them. The router starts Direction only when there is no `DESIGN.md`, and `idea` offers the first stage still to do.
- The router and our skills say to type a command like `/grill-with-docs` when the agent cannot start that skill itself, instead of promising to start it on yes.
- `idea` reads any file at the root that describes the product, keeps stack and architecture out of the brief, and asks what happens to a document that now covers the same ground.
- `design` checks and marks a screen Ready when a feature touches it, for projects whose frames were never marked. `design-check` reports a screen with no Ready frame as "not checked".
- The README says how to catch up a project you already have.
- Themes are Figma modes on one colour collection, and each screen is built once. `design` builds them that way, and `design-check` flags separate colour sets or a frame per theme, flags unbound colours as hard, and checks each theme. A screen with a copy per theme becomes one frame when a feature touches it.
- Frame names take a device when the layout differs: `<screen> · <device> — <state>`.
- Empty, loading and error are required only when a person can reach them. A state nobody can reach is recorded under Decisions in `DESIGN.md`, with the reason.
- `design-check` finds states by what a frame shows, so state names can be descriptive.
- When `init` or `update` cannot list a kit from GitHub, it says why: the network error (like `ENOTFOUND`), the HTTP status, a reply that is not JSON, or a kit with no skills. Before, a failed connection said only "Check the network".
- `design-direction` keeps Dribbble shots people valued, not just new ones. `dribbble-cards.mjs` sorts by likes, drops shots under 100 likes (`--min-likes` changes that), and prints each shot's designer. The skill ranks by likes rather than page order, compares likes between shots of a similar age, drops dated shots, and keeps at most one shot from a studio that posts near-identical concepts.

- `design-direction` lets you steer the look. It searches wide (40 to 80 candidates from every source you chose) and cuts only what is off-topic or below the source's quality floor, not for taste. The new `picker.mjs` shows that pool as one page where you mark each like or no and add why, then copy your picks into the chat. The agent reads back what your likes and noes share, runs one sharper second search from that (same designer, same tag, the same pattern on other sources), and builds the moodboard from your likes. What your noes share goes into `leave`. `dribbble-cards.mjs --picker` prints rows for it, and `sources.md` says where each source's cards keep their title, thumbnail and link. siteinspire is no longer ticked by default: it refuses scripted requests, so it cannot be in the picker.
- `init` asks which add-ons you want, in the same kind of list as the agents. `--all`, `--skill <name>` and the new `--no-addons` answer for you and skip the list. `update` never asks.
- New add-on: `modern-web-guidance`, from the Chrome team with Edge, Firefox and MDN. It steers HTML, CSS and client-side JS toward current browser APIs. It is ticked in the `init` list, and a first `init --yes` takes it. It looks each guide up with `npx`, so it needs the network; untick it, or pass `--no-addons`, if that does not suit the project. A project that already ran `init` takes it with `npx longbeard update --skill modern-web-guidance`; the agent does not offer this one.

## 0.10.0 — 2026-11-04

Long Beard is now a pipeline: Idea → Direction → Design → Architecture → Plan → Build. Each stage writes one file the next one reads (`docs/brief.md`, `design-exploration/<slug>/direction.md`, `DESIGN.md` and Ready Figma frames, `GLOSSARY.md`, specs and tickets, one PR per spec), and ends by offering the next. The `AGENTS.md` block is a stage table the agent reads to know where it is, under 200 words.

- New: `idea` grills an idea, or an existing repo, into a one-page `docs/brief.md`. `design` asks about colour and type, prototypes the brief's key screens in plain HTML, writes `DESIGN.md` in one go when you approve one, and builds the screens in Figma from it, with their empty, loading and error states. `design-check` checks a Figma screen, or a built one against its Ready frame, using the new design rules (`rules/design-rules.md`): hard findings block, soft ones are reported, and it never redesigns.
- `design-direction` replaces `design-reference-hunt`. It finds ideas and patterns, never colours or type, which are chosen in `design` on rendered screens. Cards are link, shot, pattern and why. It writes `design-exploration/<slug>/direction.md`, a proposal, instead of `.design/board.md`, and no longer asks to write `DESIGN.md`. Its sources are only ones that work without a login: Awwwards, siteinspire, Recent, Dark Mode Design, Dribbble, Muzli, and Wikimedia Commons. It brings back 12 references by default.
- `design` replaces `design-md`. An existing `DESIGN.md` stays official; `design` adds to it for a feature and never rewrites it without your yes.
- Matt Pocock's skills are a kit again: all 27, including `grill-with-docs` (Architecture) and `pr` (Build).
- Figma's `figma-use`, `figma-generate-library` and `figma-generate-design` join `figma-design-to-code`. Figma is required for a project with a UI.
- Gone: ponytail (all of it), `premortem`, `design-taste-frontend`, `design-md`, `design-reference-hunt`.
- `animate`, `jury` and `web-design-guidelines` are add-ons now, with `security-audit` and `image-to-code`. The agent offers one when it is needed. `init` and `update` take one with `--skill <name>`, or all with `--all`. An add-on you already have stays and keeps refreshing.
- `update` removes any skill the catalog or its kit no longer lists, not only kit skills. One you edited stays unless you pass `--force`.
- `capture.mjs` takes `--width` (below 768 it shoots a phone at 2x), `--cookie` for a page behind a login, and `--strict`, which exits 1 when the page lands at another address.

People who already ran `init`:

```bash
npm install --save-dev @jonandev/longbeard@latest
npx longbeard update            # installs the new skills, removes the dropped ones you did not edit
npx longbeard update --force    # only if update listed dropped skills it kept because you edited them
```

`.design/` is no longer read. Keep it for your own reference or delete it; new explorations go to `design-exploration/`, which `design-direction` adds to `.gitignore`.

## 0.7.0 — 2026-10-30

- The hunt is smaller. Its goal is one `DESIGN.md`, and `.design/` is scratch you may delete once that exists; a later hunt for one piece works without it. `SKILL.md` drops from about 400 lines to under 200, and `quality.md` and `shots.md` become one `sources.md`. `proof.mjs` is gone: a prototype of the gaps is the proof. `moodboard.mjs` runs once, before the pick, as a grid of shots linked to their sources; `--leads`, `--also`, `--why`, `--count`, and `--imagery` are gone, and the pick is said in chat. A board card is six lines. A later hunt writes `.design/hunts/<slug>/board.md` with the same cards and its own moodboard.
- `tdd` from [Matt Pocock](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) is in the default kit, under Code. It runs only when you ask for TDD, test-first, or red-green-refactor; `ponytail` stays the default for building and fixing. People who already ran `init`: install this version, then run `npx longbeard update`.
- The `AGENTS.md` block `init` and `update` write now matches the 0.6.0 hunt: the `DESIGN.md` ask is its own message, asked once more if they move on without answering. 0.6.0 shipped the older "ask once" line. Run `npx longbeard update` after installing this release.
- `update` tells an edit by content, not by file time. A fresh clone or a checkout no longer makes every skill look edited, so they refresh again. A lock written by 0.6.0 or earlier has no hash yet: if `update` lists a skill under "your edits" that you did not change, run `npx longbeard update --force` once.
- A direction can hold behaviour. **Read this** has a `behaviour:` line (how the page moves, pages, fades, or reveals, or `none`), `DESIGN.md` has a `## Behaviour` section, and `animate` reads it first.
- `capture.mjs` writes the format its `--out` extension names (PNG for `.png`). Given an image file, `--clip` is in the image's own pixels.
- `dribbble-cards.mjs` also drops code, video, and photo editors, AI assistants, dashboards, and SaaS.
- The hunt's first message has a redo line for when `DESIGN.md` already exists, and one progress line is allowed when the search ends.
- After the `DESIGN.md` question, the hunt offers a next step instead of stopping: prototype the gaps (the gaps are the prototype's brief), hunt one, or stop. For an app, the first round also asks for the main screens and how you move between them, and 6 references is offered as a quick look.
- `DESIGN.md` names ideas, not sources: no reference names, no `.design/` paths, and `leave` is written as ideas. It gains a "To see it working" line and a **Decisions** list. `design-md` may now record decisions you approve on a rendered prototype, with the reason, the date, and a contrast check for every new colour token.
- `.design/` stays out of git. The hunt adds it to `.gitignore` on its first write and says why: the screenshots are other people's work. If it was already committed, it tells you, and leaves untracking it to you.
- An `ownable` element is unconfirmed until it has been seen on a rendered screen. Faces are set at their real sizes before they are kept: the italic at quote size, and the figures where numbers sit.
- The router tells `design-taste-frontend` that with `DESIGN.md` it builds and checks, and does not art-direct; it also takes a prototype ask. A Figma file stays with `figma-design-to-code`. App screens use taste's app-screen rules and inspector, and a warning about something `DESIGN.md` decided is reported, not "fixed"; accessibility failures are still fixed. If you already have a `DESIGN.md`, move any reference names in it into ideas.

## 0.6.0 — 2026-10-23

- `update` refreshes a kit skill from the installed package. It used to hand every refresh to the skills CLI, which cannot fetch a skill that ships inside this package, so it printed "up to date" over files from an older release. Each line now says which versions moved (`design-reference-hunt 0.5.1 → 0.6.0`), `--force` always replaces, and the last line names the version you are running. Install the package first; `update` still does not fetch it.
- `design-reference-hunt` opens the moodboard **before** the pick, with eight to twelve screens rather than two, every picture linked to its source, captions under the shot, and whole references instead of a 320 px crop. New card fields carry it: `tagline:` (about six words, the only text on a tile), `faces:` (names only, so the type tile sets them for real), and `slot:` (a product page stays off the moodboard). After the pick it runs again with `--leads` and `--why`, so the lead's caption is the reason they gave, not a line written before they chose. `--imagery` is opt-in: a look with no pictures in it shows no imagery tiles, and an imagery count of 0 is a complete board.
- `design-reference-hunt` adapts a colour for a reason — contrast, the twist, our own pictures, our own brand — and keeps a generic sampled cream or ink as it is. Nudging a hex so it differs is a change nobody can see.
- `scripts/dribbble-cards.mjs` reads likes and views from the card's own stat elements and prints both columns. It used to take the first number in the card text, which could be the likes count, a digit from the title, or a full stop. A count it cannot read stays empty.
- `scripts/capture.mjs` takes a `file://` URL, so the moodboard can be checked before anyone is shown it, and `--clip-selector "<css>"` clips to an element's box instead of guessing coordinates for a type specimen.
- `design-reference-hunt` starts a whole look with a conversation, not a search. One round, every question skippable with "go": what they are into, pictures they already like (kept as `source: user` seeds that steer and rank the search), the subject and surface with the identity nouns from the name, whether it needs pictures at all, a checklist of sources with defaults for the surface, and how many references to see. It never asks a second round before searching.
- The search is smaller and cleaner. It queries for the form, not the word, and `dribbble-cards.mjs` drops a shared negative list (tees, CVs, worksheets, posters, logos) by slug and title before any thumbnail is fetched, then prints the noise rate. An app stops when two more queries add nothing, keeps the number they chose, and opens every shortlisted shot at full size. App hunts go to shipped screens first and mark each card `shipped: yes | concept`. Cards are short; only the lead and the `also` cards get the full fields.
- The moodboard is the narrowing step. It is built from what was found, before they answer, and the question is "which leads, which do you also like, which feel wrong, and why?". `--also` takes several cards, `--count` sets how many references show.
- **Read this** gains `ownable` (one concrete element from the project's own identity that the lead does not have), `gaps` (key screens with no strong reference, each offered as a later hunt after the persist answer) and `not shown` (what a concept lead never faced). `design-md` carries `ownable` and `gaps` into `DESIGN.md` and sends a board without `ownable` back.
- New `scripts/proof.mjs` writes `.design/proof.html` from **Read this**: paper and night side by side, a list row and a phone column, the faces at body size, contrast ratios, and text from the project's own README or product file. It is not the product. It runs before the persist ask and asks whether fewer font families would do.
- A redo of the whole look uses at least one source the last board did not, and one deliberately opposite query, to test whether the lead still wins.
- People who already ran `init` get all of this by installing this release, then running `npx longbeard update`.
